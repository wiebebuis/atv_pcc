"**Meditativo**" can be seen as a synonym of "musingly". It indicates *thinking*, maybe *meditative exploration* of musical material coming from the Dowland song. It also made me think of "*ricercare le corde*", according to GROOVE the root meaning of the **ricercare** genre. This was a current genre in Dowland's time, a genre which departs from a musical idea or "**soggetto**" (subject).  

My  impression of this movement is one of searching, sometimes hesitant, without a clear focus or direction. The dynamic is overall (very) soft: it varies from ppp to pp. 

What could be the intention/purpose of this movement? 
I propose the following:

"Let's see what we've got in terms of musical material and what we can do with it". But wait a minute: How do you mean "**see**"? It's **dark**. We have to use our *sense of touch*... we have to move in a careful manner, on tiptoes so to speak.

This movement functions as a **prelude** in which the essence of the musical material of the coming movements is presented (for the greater part) melodically; there are two harmonic moments.
Although we have barlines, there is no time signature and the rhythm is free and in a way "quasi recitativo".

#### Segmentation
This movement has a strophic character; there are in total 9 segments, all ending with a long note, comparable to the line endings in the song. Segment 6 and 9 are the only two where harmony plays a role. We have an Eb9 chord without 7th, the F falls to C. There is no sense of resolution and no suggestion of a key; therefore it's better to consider it as a **polychord**:  Fm over E, where G# = Ab. This is a common phenomenon in the music of Britten. 

In this movement the important/characteristic motives from the Dowland song can be recognised, but they are distorted. There is no sense of a key. There is a lack of focus and direction in this movement. I think this is a deliberate choice by Britten. He may be paraphrasing a song of Dowland: " in darkness I make you dwell".
